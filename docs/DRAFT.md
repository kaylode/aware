# Adapting In-Context Learners for Longitudinal Electronic Patient Records: A TabPFN Perspective

## Abstract:

Recent advances in in-context learning (ICL) for tabular data, exemplified by the TabPFN framework, have demonstrated state-of-the-art performance on small datasets without explicit training. However, these models remain underexplored in high-stakes, temporally structured domains such as healthcare, where data are sparse, irregular, and longitudinal in nature. In this work, we investigate the adaptation of TabPFN and its scalable variants—including TabPFN-ICD, TuneTables, and TabICL—to longitudinal electronic patient records (EPR). We propose a principled approach to flatten temporally distributed patient trajectories into tabular representations, leveraging feature engineering, time-aware augmentations, and structural model modifications. Across multiple predictive tasks on real-world clinical datasets, including MIMIC-IV, our adapted TabPFN models outperform or rival state-of-the-art baselines such as CatBoost, FTTransformer, and sequence models like Mamba. We further analyze the robustness, interpretability, and causal reasoning potential of TabPFN in clinical settings. This work is the first to bridge ICL-based tabular modeling with longitudinal healthcare data, offering a scalable and interpretable alternative to traditional deep learning pipelines.
